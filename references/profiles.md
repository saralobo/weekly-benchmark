# Recommended profiles

Use these to **arrive with a hypothesis** instead of a form. After reading the product's site, pick the closest profile and propose the whole configuration at once: *"From the site this looks like B2B SaaS; I'd suggest these tracks, these dimensions and these sources — what changes?"*

People correct faster than they answer. A wrong profile they adjust beats ten blank questions.

---

## B2B SaaS
**Tracks:** competitors, innovation
**Dimensions:** pricing, product, positioning
**Visual sources:** Land-book, Mobbin (if there's a plan)
**Why:** the fight is over pricing and promise. Price moves slowly and every move is a strong signal. Visuals matter less than argument.
**Typical trigger:** any competitor touches the entry tier or the billing model.

## Consumer app
**Tracks:** competitors, visual
**Dimensions:** product, reputation, content
**Visual sources:** Mobbin, Dribbble, Refero
**Why:** UI convention is genuinely competitive here, and store reviews are the best leading indicator there is.
**Typical trigger:** a competitor ships a new onboarding flow or changes the paywall.

## E-commerce
**Tracks:** competitors, visual, innovation
**Dimensions:** pricing, content, reputation
**Visual sources:** Land-book, Awwwards, ArchDaily if there are physical stores
**Why:** price and campaign turn over weekly. This is the profile where a weekly cadence pays off most.
**Typical trigger:** aggressive promotion, free shipping, changed returns policy.

## Creative or design product
**Tracks:** visual, innovation
**Dimensions:** positioning, content
**Visual sources:** Savee, Behance, Awwwards, Dribbble
**Why:** the currency here is repertoire. The weekly value sits in the gallery, not in a pricing diff.
**Typical trigger:** less about competitors, more about a visual language becoming the default.

## Hardware or physical product
**Tracks:** competitors, innovation
**Dimensions:** product, pricing, reputation
**Visual sources:** Dezeen, Divisare
**Why:** slow cycle, so the value sits in supply chain, regulation and patents — the innovation track carries the report.
**Typical trigger:** next-generation announcement, regulatory change, supply disruption.

## Local service
**Tracks:** competitors
**Dimensions:** pricing, reputation
**Visual sources:** ArchDaily if there's a physical space
**Why:** the field is small and known. Short report, focused on price and on what customers complain about.
**Typical trigger:** a new entrant in the area, a price change.

---

## How to use this in conversation

1. Read the site. Classify.
2. Propose the whole profile in one message, **with the reasoning behind each choice**.
3. Ask what changes — not item by item, all at once.
4. **Don't offer to enable everything.** Three tracks across six sources makes a long round and a scattered report. Say so when they ask for all of it.

If the product fits no profile, build one from scratch — profiles are a shortcut, not a cage.
