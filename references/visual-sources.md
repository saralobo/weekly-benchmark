# Visual source catalogue

Show this during setup when the `visual` track is on. **Recommend 3 to 5**, never all — each source adds round time, and a good gallery comes from the right sources, not many sources.

What makes a source worth using is knowing **what to look for there and what not to**. Wrong source for the question produces a beautiful, useless wall.

---

## Mobbin
**Real product UI patterns** — 621k screens and 142k flows from apps that shipped.
- **Look for:** how real apps solve onboarding, paywalls, empty states, permissions, upsell. Complete flows, not isolated screens.
- **Don't look for:** visual daring. It's real product, so it's conservative by nature.
- **Access:** official MCP at `https://api.mobbin.com/mcp`. **Requires a paid plan** (Pro, from ~€10/month). See `connectors.md`.
- **Irreplaceable when:** you're designing a flow and need to know what's already convention.

## Awwwards
**Web design at the edge** — interaction, motion, experimental typography.
- **Look for:** the ceiling of what the web can do today. Transitions, scroll, WebGL.
- **Don't look for:** usability. Plenty of award-winning sites are gorgeous and hard to use.
- **Careful:** copying Awwwards into a working product usually goes badly. Use it as a source of ambition, not of pattern.

## Behance
**Complete projects** — branding, art direction, whole visual systems.
- **Look for:** project reasoning. Cases with process, palette, application. Good for identity and brand architecture.
- **Don't look for:** product UI that works. Much of it is concept that never shipped.

## Dribbble
**Execution detail** — components, micro-interactions, finish.
- **Look for:** how someone solved a button, a picker, a chart. Fine grain.
- **Don't look for:** whole products. It's the source with the widest gap between what you see and what's buildable.

## Savee
**Raw visual curation** — image, graphics, editorial, fashion, objects.
- **Look for:** repertoire beyond the screen. Colour, texture, composition, atmosphere.
- **Don't look for:** UI. Not the subject there.
- **Worth it when:** the question is art direction rather than interface.

## Land-book · Godly · SiteInspire
**Landing pages and product sites.**
- **Look for:** how products like yours present themselves. Page structure, argument hierarchy, social proof.
- Land-book is best organised by category; Godly is the most curated; SiteInspire the broadest.

## Refero · Screenlane
**UI patterns by category**, web and mobile.
- **Look for:** the open alternative to Mobbin when there's no paid plan. Smaller coverage, same nature.

## ArchDaily · Divisare · Dezeen
**Architecture, space and objects.**
- **Look for:** built work, plans, materials, light. Divisare is the most visually curated; ArchDaily the most complete; Dezeen covers product design too.
- **Worth it when:** benchmarking physical retail, workspaces, stands, and anything that exists in three dimensions.

---

## Credit rule

Every reference from any source carries **author, project name and link**. Never crop out a watermark, never present someone else's image without attribution. It's repertoire with provenance — and that's what separates a benchmark from a scrapbook.

Reports containing a gallery should stay **private by default**. They're working reference, not publication.
